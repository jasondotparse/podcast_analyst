MARCUS OYELARAN: Partly. I don't disagree that 1.6T ramps hard. Where I'd push back is on how long the pricing stays rational. From the buyer's side, the playbook is always the same. PRIYA RAMAN: That's fair, and it's in my model. I assume 1.6T pricing starts declining in the back half of 2027. MARCUS OYELARAN: Maybe. I just think people underestimate how aggressive procurement teams get when the spend is this large. DANA WHITFIELD: Priya, what are the risks you worry about with Corvane, if you're the bull?

DANA WHITFIELD: Is Talmadge adding capacity?

MARCUS OYELARAN: They are. They're building out a new fab in Malaysia, and from what I've heard it comes online in the second half of 2027. PRIYA RAMAN: I'd say Corvane has done more than most to lock up supply. They signed a multi-year capacity agreement with Talmadge last year. But Marcus is right that nobody is fully insulated.

DANA WHITFIELD: And underneath the lasers there's a materials story too.

MARCUS OYELARAN: Yes, indium phosphide. The EML lasers are grown on indium phosphide substrates, and there are only a handful of substrate suppliers in the world. The latest quotes I've seen are closer to 40 weeks. Two years ago it was about 12 weeks. So it has more than tripled. When a laser maker wants to add capacity, the substrate order is one of the very first things they have to place, and it's now most of a year out.

DANA WHITFIELD: That's a long lead time for something so foundational.

MARCUS OYELARAN: It is. And it's a quiet risk because it doesn't show up in anyone's earnings call until it's already a problem.

DANA WHITFIELD: Let's talk about co-packaged optics, because that's the thing I hear about at every conference. PRIYA RAMAN: Long term it's a real shift, but I don't think it's a near-term risk. Corvane has its own co-packaged optics program, and their light engines could end up inside those packages. But the bigger point is timing.

MARCUS OYELARAN: I'll be blunt on timing. I don't see co-packaged optics hitting volume adoption until 2028 at the earliest. With a pluggable module, if a laser fails, a technician pulls the module and slots in a new one in a couple of minutes. With co-packaged optics, a failed laser can mean replacing the whole switch. Operations teams hate that.

DANA WHITFIELD: So pluggables have a few more years.

MARCUS OYELARAN: At least a few more years as the volume product, yes. Co-packaged optics will show up first in specific, very high density deployments, and grow from there.

PRIYA RAMAN: I agree with that. I model co-packaged optics as less than 5% of the optical interconnect market in 2027. It's a 2028 and beyond story.
